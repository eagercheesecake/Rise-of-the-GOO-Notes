### Logline
*The Bagworm grows interested in the party's actions.*
### Synopsis
Hadrian heals Helen's tongue and Brewster's finger. The party hires Holliday and provides her with ingredients for a poison. Reptile gains funds to enroll his siblings in the Lyceum. Branwyn gets a book telling a fable about Disgaia. The party meets Mephistopheles the Bard, who asks them to free him from his contract with the Bagworm. Martin follows one of the Bagworm's lackeys to some barracks, where he finds a note from Gwyneth asking him to kill the party. Moiloc and Brewster meet the sultan, and Nissa joins the party. Brewster gets a ring from the sultan, which combines with two other rings to form Disgaia's wedding ring. The group kills the Bagworm, surviving the powerful death knight gifted to him by Gwyneth. Branwyn gets the deed to the Cat's Paw and other treasures from the Bagworm's office. Mephistopheles questions the Bagworm's corpse on the party's behalf. Martin, Brewster, and Lucretia open the Tower of Erebus.
# Day 61
### Sellsword’s Sell-Swords
[[Martin Sootspot|Martin]], [[Goro Hattori|Goro]], and [[Lucretia]] continue to the mercenary guild, hoping to ask [[Branwyn of Coryphea|Branwyn]] about her book with the god of secrets without a name, thinking it may help open the Tower of Erebus. He sees some people entering the [[Sultan Anu|sultan's]] palace, assuming he has court today. Lucretia explains that he holds court once a month, where people can express their grievances. Sometimes he gives items if he is able, which is how Lucretia got her necklace.

At Sellsword’s, Martin enters. [[Tarren Hammerfist|Tarren]] is looking at a mercenary board for bounties, trying to stay away from awkward conversation. Martin realizes that [[Moiloc Kodlaksdottër|Moiloc]] has found a mercenary to hire, but the mercenary hall is pretty empty. Moiloc tells Martin about her fight with the orc, saying it was an easy battle, causing Martin to remember the puddle of viscera he passed. Moiloc says how she dealt with the orc was just common training from the Kodlak Islands. Moiloc claims everyone clapped. Martin constructs a scenario of Moiloc reducing a man to mush as everyone around her cheers.

[[Holliday]] interrupts, asking if Martin has the money to hire her, or knows more information on the plan. Holliday recognizes him as Martin Sootspot, insinuating this means the job must pay well. Martin says the plan requires some discretion, and Holliday asks where they’re staying. Martin says they’re at the Cat’s Paw, and Holliday says she isn’t welcome there anymore, and they could talk at the Rusty Spoon. Holliday takes a 50g advance to discuss the details there.

Holliday leads them to the Cat’s Paw, leaving Moiloc and [[Reptile]] behind to wait for Branwyn and Brewster. Martin tries to talk to Tarren about the price of a mercenary, who says it normally costs around 600g.
### The Mausoleum of Dario the Twin

### The Rusty Spoon
Holliday, Martin, Lucretia, Goro, and Tarren arrive at the Rusty Spoon. Martin recognizes the person playing the piano was the same [[Mephistopheles the Bard|bard]] from the Cat’s Paw, from the night prior. Martin tells Holliday that they are gathering information for when their reinforcements arrive, so that together they can free the slaves. Holliday assumes they are going to kill someone, and asks how many targets they have. Martin only mentions Sister Marie. Holliday says she has experience in killing nobles. She asks for a price point of 800g. She also says she needs a few bushels of a thimbleweed, which grows nearby to do the job. The agreement lands that they will give her 400g now, and 400g after the job is complete. They can pick her up the next day at the Rusty Spoon, but they will need to give her the thimbleweed beforehand so she can prepare. As they shake hands, Martin notices that the bard has been eying them during their conversation.

Martin continues to the bard, who says he overheard them talking about freeing the slaves, asking if they can free him from [[the Bagworm]] first. The Bagworm has him under “residency,” which is actually keeping him in the city under his employ, lest the Bagworm take his feet and hands. He has a divination sigil, which tracks him, sending it to the Bagworm’s ring. Bushido talks to Martin, saying they must help him. Martin says he’ll see what he can do.

### Sultan's Palace
Ishtar is able to get the group to the front of the line to see the sultan. A human scrambles out, running, leaving a trail of blood. Ishtar claims that he may have asked for a job. Being a warlord, the sultan likes to have people prove their worth via a one-on-one battle to the death with one of the sultan’s soldiers. Moiloc and Brewster enter alone.

The sultan is a handsome lion man, but notably wears no crown. Moiloc gives a speech, bordering a business pitch or political speech, to the sultan, saying they beat the bandit camp, and would like some help freeing the slaves, ending with saying they have news of his brother. The sultan ignores everything except the news of his brother. The sultan says it’s good he’s alive, maybe now he can kill him himself. He calls for Nissa, a tabaxi woman with plate armor. He says she is a whelp in need of experience, asking them to take her with them. Nissa protests, but Moiloc accepts. He tosses a pouch toward Moiloc, containing a writ of ownership of Nissa, saying she will be under Moiloc’s control until she has proven her worth in battle. The sultan asks Brewster how he plans to take down the slave system. Brewster says he has escaped them before, and has invaluable experience that will help him beat them again.

The sultan asks Brewster to follow him, saying he has a gift that will come at a price. Moiloc is left with a very angry Nissa. Moiloc tries to cool things over, but Nissa remains upset. Moiloc says as a daughter of a noble, she understands Nissa’s struggles.

Brewster follows the sultan down a long hallway, leading him to a portcullis, which the sultan opens by hand. Inside is an armory with countless pieces of armor, and a gnarled, wooden shield, with three circles on its center. Brewster can tell the shield is insanely magical, possibly an artifact of the gods. The sultan took it from the previous sultan, 20 years ago. The sultan reached into the circles, pulling out a ring of wood. He gives the ring of wood to Brewster, saying it will help him on his journey. Brewster asks what the price the sultan spoke of it, and the sultan says he wants Whitehall. Brewster asks if he will be using slavery, and the sultan says slavery tends to not turn out well for the ruler’s longevity.

Brewster takes the ring and casts Identify. It is a divine artifact, although slightly worn. Carved into the inside of the ring is a fleur-de-lis. The sultan also gives Brewster a sending stone, saying to let him know once he has cleared out Whitehall, and leave Nissa in Whitehall. He also gives Brewster and his party access to the armory.

Brewster shows the ring to Branwyn, asking to look in her book for information on it. It seems like it’s exuding heat. Branwyn feels the ring pull toward her wooden ring, and asks for Brewster’s Ring of Rot. They coalesce into one ring, and three stone eyes open on it, darting around. Brewster casts Identify again, identifying it as Withermold. When worn, it gives the wearer advantage on Charm saving throws, cannot be detected by divination magic, and can cast Commune with Nature and Foresight.

### The Cat's Paw
Branwyn takes the rings from Martin and disguises herself as the Bagworm. The bottom side of a table has indents where the rings can be inserted. Branwyn does so, and the couch moves, revealing a lot of gold, and a deed to the Cat’s Paw. The bard thanks Martin, and introduces himself as Mephistopheles.

Mephistopheles asks some questions. The Bagworm says his master and Gwyneth are different people. Gwyneth gave him the death knight. His master was the hooded figure, and they can find him in Whitehall. Holliday was banned due to personal differences.

The Fleur-de-Lis book is called the Mysteries of Gravewatch, and seems to be a fictional recounting of the desecration of Coryphea. It speaks of a witch who, alongside a wizard, killed Disgaia, the previous god of nature. The goddess died and was consumed by a giant gaping maw, and transformed into a living tree. Disgaia no longer exists on this plane, save for one artifact from when she was human: her wedding ring. When the ring is reformed, Disgaia herself will be brought back into the world. However, this is framed as something which should not happen, saying the death of Disgaia was a boon on the world, the earth will sunder and shake the ground beneath your feet. The book seems older than the actual fall of Coryphea, and doesn’t seem to make sense.

Martin demands that Branwyn give her the book with the missing name of a god, and take it to the Tower of Erebus. Branwyn is tired, so Martin and Brewster go alone. Everyone else sleeps at the Cat’s Paw. When they open the book, the word Shurima that Branwyn wrote in, is erased, and replaced with [[Shurima the Corpsemaker|Xerxes]], and the tower begins to shake.
