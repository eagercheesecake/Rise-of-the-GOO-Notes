| [[Session 31 - Fourteen Rings]] |              |
### Logline
Brewster sits in a throne. Moiloc and Kaldrake go to a casino. Branwyn is upset.
### Synopsis
Brewster enters the Tower of Erebus and sits on a throne, being taken to the Ringed City, where Shurima has taken over. Martin tries to enter the Tower of Erebus, but is teleported to the moon. He is merged with Kaldrake while there, and finds Gulvayas. Kaldrake's soul is lost when Martin throws a dagger at the dome containing Gulvayas, and goes into Gulvayas's bindings. Brewster destroys the book that opens the Tower of Erebus. Branwyn gives the deed to the Cat's Paw to Hadrian and receives Dario's autobiography. Moiloc kills a street robber and robs the corpse of his target. Moiloc, Kaldrake, and Gulvayas burn down and rob a gambling den. The party has an intervention. The group arrives in Whitehall, and buy a house.
# Day 61
### Tower of Erebus
The door to the tower of Erebus opens to a long hallway, devoid of light except for a blue light at the end of the hall. [[Lothar (Brewster) Nash|Brewster]] feels a sense of dread, although he feels as if he’d be safe. [[Martin Sootspot|Martin]] feels excited. There’s a wooden doorframe at the end with a portal of energy, which seems familiar to Martin. Brewster convinces Martin to lead.

Martin enters first, and finds himself in a case, sloping up to an exit. He walks out of the cave, finding himself face to face with a green orb floating in darkness. Looking around, he realizes he is on the moon. He’s wearing robes, and recognizes himself as being [[Kaldrake Sootspot|Kaldrake]].

Brewster and [[Lucretia]] also enter the portal, and find themselves inside the same black hallway, with runes along the wall leading to a staircase leading down. The staircase leads to a circular room, with the runes spiraling into a circle on the ground. Inside the circle are mummified kneeling corpses, bowing toward the room’s center, with two thrones, one growing from the other. Above the throne is the mummified corpse of a dragon strung up on the ceiling. Carved into the back of the throne is an eyeball. Lucretia finds hints of necrosis where the dragon’s flesh is falling off the bone. The dragon is frozen in animation, as if time has stopped.

Brewster believes this to be a ritual room, used for a large conjuration spell. Brewster’s Detect Magic finds that the magic there is long gone. There is still conjuration energy present, lingering, so the spell must have been ginormous. Brewster tries to convince Lucretia to join him in the chair, saying Martin would want them to, but she refuses. Brewster sits in the throne, feeling the residual magic beginning to kickstart itself. The world shifts and shakes, and Brewster blinks, finding himself in a familiar field under the Ringed City.
### The Ringed City
The City doesn’t look the same as Brewster remembers it, it’s not as bright as before. It’s crumbling, and shrouded in darkness. The bridge of light exists, but when Brewster gets to the door, he finds it closed. On the door is two eyes, one being Shurima’s eye sigil, and one being the eye on Helen’s back. Brewster casts Gaseous Form, slipping through a crumbling form in the side of the wall. The city looks very different: instead of a spiral there are just stone mausoleums surrounding a giant tower. The mausoleums are all accounted for, as if they were cloned.

[[Gal Troxic]], [[Styx the Pathfinder|Styx]], the [[Unknown God of Chaos|unknown god]] with the obelisk, [[Maokai]], [[Sekollah]], [[Laogzed]], [[Olidamuru]], and [[Shurima the Corpsemaker|Xerxes]] (newly discovered as the true god of secrets) each have one hadozee guarding the mausoleum, but the others are empty. The door to the tower in the center opens as Brewster approaches, causing the remaining hadozee to panic and run into their mausoleums. Brewster enters into [[Disgaia|Disgaia's]] mausoleum to hide, finding it barren except for two tapestries showing Disgaia’s symbol. In the corner is a plain stone sarcophagus, with a dryad of a woman made of bark. On the floor, in front of the sarcophagus is a dead hadozee. Brewster believes the hadozee has been dead for an uncountable amount of time.

Brewster peeks out, realizing that there is a trail of smoke coming out of the top of the tower. It forms words in the sky, not dissipating, saying “come in.” Brewster approaches the tower, and notices the tower growing as he approaches, like a hyperbolic space. As Brewster enters the door, he finds himself in a large amphitheater, with a throne in the center, surrounded by sixteen smaller thrones and eight daises. He sees a heavily muscled figure with blue skin, fully bandaged. He has one yellow eye.

Brewster’s voice echoes throughout the chamber as he asked why he was called there. Shurima beckons him, and Brewster feels himself drawn to him, but resists. Brewster tries to leave, but Shurima stops him. Brewster sighs and sits down. Shurima looks disappointed and floats over to Brewster, sitting across from him. Shurima says he didn’t bring him here this time. He also says it’s been a long time since someone sat in his chair. Shurima asks why Brewster is there, saying he had hoped to summon someone else, and asking Brewster to bring his friend into the tower. Shurima paces a bit, thinking, before saying he has no need for Brewster, and teleports Brewster back.

Brewster returns to the room and recognizes them as the same runes surrounding Brasov. Brewster copies down the Celestial writing so he can show Martin and have him read it.
### The Moon
Meanwhile, Martin regains Kaldrake’s memories while on the moon, mixing with Martin’s memories. He looks around, seeing a dome of light on another moon. He jumps across to the other moon, recognizing [[Gulvayas Hakon|Gulvayas]] as being shackled in the dome. His mouth is sewn shut, and he shakes his head in fear as Martin approaches. Martin says he isn’t there to hurt him, and might be his great-grandson. Martin casts Detect Thoughts, and only detects fear and one thought, “son,” as he violently thrashes at Martin trying to attack him. The word is kinder than his actions. Martin realizes that Gulvayas can’t understand him, but seems to have recognized the word “Sootspot.” Martin notices a very powerful enchantment spell on Gulvayas’s shackles, with two gemstones imbedded on each shackle. Both gems on his left wrist are shining, but only one gem on the right. There are also two dimmed stones on his ankle restraints. Martin finds the dome to have some sort of absorbing property, and realizes he shouldn’t cast magic on the dome.

Martin throws a dagger at the dome, and shatters against the dome. Martin watches as a piece of Kaldrake’s soul is ripped out from within him, and flows into a gemstone on his right wrist restraint. The shackles dissipate, and Gulvayas’s form darkens, his eyes going black. The Detect Thoughts gains a new word: “brother.” Martin sees fine lines on the wrist, like a doll, fluttering in and out. Martin decides to leave, and jumps off the moon, reappearing at the outside of the Tower of Erebus.
### The Cat's Paw
Martin runs to catch up to Brewster and explains what happened on the moon. Brewster stalls a bit while leading Martin to the Cat’s Paw, and has Martin read the celestial, revealing it is a conjuration spell: exactly the same as the one in Brasov, leading them to wonder if Kaldrake was really revived. They then look through the book that got them into the tower, and when Brewster confirms they have gotten all the information from it, throws it in the fire, before filling Martin in on what happened. Martin says it’s up to Brewster to explain that to [[Branwyn of Coryphea|Branwyn]]. It’s almost morning, and the pair decide to sleep through the morning.
# Day 62
### The Cat's Paw
In the morning, Branwyn wakes up and talks to [[Nissa]], asking who she thinks is best to give the deed to the Cat’s Paw to. Nissa suggests [[Sultan Anu]], but is curious on why she wouldn’t want to keep it. [[Holliday]] overhears this, and says she will take the deed in lieu of giving it to the sultan, warning of giving the sultan enough power for a borderline dictatorship. This convinces Branwyn to give it to the clerics to restore it to a chapel, much to Holliday’s dismay.
### The Mausoleum of Darion the Twin
Branwyn goes to give the deed to [[Hadrian]], who agrees to use it to improve Velbrass. Hadrian gives her one of Dario’s books, saying it is one of the only remaining books of his that exist. It’s an autobiography of Dario, detailing growing up as the son of a slave, his life of freeing slaves and killing slave lords. It also mentions the great dragon war, but doesn’t provide that much information, as he didn’t survive. It says Dario allied himself with several guards to fight the mortal empire of Shurima: [[Sha-Sĭme]], [[Celica the Good|Celica the Twin]], Disgaia, and [[Hortensia]]. There is a small passage detailing Disgaia’s retreat from the world. Branwyn gives it to [[Ishtar]], who is very grateful, and tosses another book away to make room.
### Velbrass
Moiloc wakes up and wanders the streets to find breakfast, and buys some fried cactus for the trip. Moiloc sees a tabaxi gutted in an alley and confronts the attacker. He holds his knife to Moiloc, motioning to the gold sack in the dead tabaxi’s hands, telling her to back up, and that the gold is his. He leans over to pick it up, and Moiloc lifts her sword to knock him out. She drops it, hands still slippery from the cactus. The tabaxi stabs her. Moiloc casts Eldritch Blast, blasting a hole through his head. She takes the gold and leaves.
### The Cat's Paw
Martin and Brewster wake up. Brewster finds Branwyn giving archery tips to [[William Archer]]. Brewster pulls Branwyn into a room and tells her about his time in the tower. Branwyn says they shouldn’t be keeping things from Moiloc, since the best way to stop her from being so untrustworthy likely isn’t to lie to her. They agree to tell her about the war with Shurima detailed in Dario’s book, but Brewster is able to get Branwyn to keep the story about Shurima and the tower secret, for Moiloc’s own safety.
### Gambling Den
Meanwhile, [[Mephistopheles the Bard|Mephistopheles]] tells Martin and Moiloc that he owes someone 100g in gambling debts. They get to the door of the gambling den, and Mephistopheles gives the password: “soft hands, easy pockets.” The guard opens the door and makes them drop their weapons. Moiloc tries to get him to let her keep her sword since it follows her oath to Shurima, but he doesn’t allow it. She leaves it, and they continue down the stairs into the gambling den.

Mephistopheles points out to Martin a tabaxi woman who he owes the money to. Moiloc casts Charm Person to try and get her to call off the debt. She says she’ll call it off for a favor in the future, but Moiloc declines and pays the 100g. They leave the gambling hall, and Mephistopheles pleads his thanks. Moiloc asks Mephistopheles if he wants to kill them, and he agrees that they would have a lot of money. The crew return to the top of the stairs and grab their weapons. Moiloc tells Mephistopheles to distract the guard. While he’s performing, Moiloc knocks him out with the butt of her sword. Mephistopheles says they could just end the problem, and Moiloc feels her pendant pulse. She gives in, and slits his throat.

Mephistopheles suggests they simple cast Fireball inside the room and just end them all in one go. Moiloc says they shouldn’t destroy anything of value, but Mephistopheles says gold isn’t flammable. They agree, and Martin casts Fireball inside the room, erupting it into flame. They hear screams, and all fourteen people immediately die. Moiloc says authorities are coming and they need to loot and scoot. When they return, they lie to Brewster saying the building coincidentally exploded right after they paid the gold, so they went and got it back.
### The Cat's Paw
Meanwhile, Branwyn and Brewster talk to [[Helen Berestova|Helen]]. Her family is from Maedros, she grew up in Wallachia. She married her husband, Artyom, when she was 19, and was married to him for two years. When Lothric was overrun, she was knocked unconscious, and woke up bound and tongueless in the dungeon. Branwyn catches that she was lying about what she was doing in town, and presses her on it. She explains that she is a spy from Maedros, sent to report on Gwyneth. She asks Branwyn not to tell anyone, as she is promised not to tell. Branwyn agrees to keep her secret, if Helen tells her why they were looking into her. Helen says the Architect is in power in Maedros, and he may have wanted her to get information on Mora. Helen also admits that she’s 27 years old. Branwyn asks Helen to be more forthcoming, and she says she will let Branwyn see her reports before she sends them. Helen then returns to Martin and asks for more money, even though she’s decked out in new armor, and Martin gives in.

Branwyn tells Brewster about Helen, asking him to remember [[Contigo Muñoz|Contigo]], the last person they knew from Maedros, hinting that she’s a spy without saying it. Brewster thanks her for her honesty, and tells her Moiloc’s story of what happened at the gambling den. Branwyn runs downstairs after Martin and Moiloc and fully tells them off, having a long, angry talk with them about being a good team going into their most dangerous mission yet. Eventually, Mephistopheles announces that [[Ulysses]] has arrived. On his belt is a shrunken head. Branwyn sees her lock of hair sprouting out of it. The group, and Vulcan, lead him to the Bagworm’s office to discuss the plan.
## The Aethereum
Diggle meets the team of sixteen at the Lyceum and leads them to the teleporter. The group re-appears on the inside of a mountain, in Fort Endrio. It is filled to the brim with armed Inquisitors. The room has no windows, and is made of stone. Down a small set of stairs is a woman in leathers, with a tattoo of a normal, human eye on her forehead. She looks the group up and down, and asks who the leader is. Holliday says there are setting up an estate for the McMasters. The woman says they can fill out paperwork, and provides nine collars for the slaves. She says Brewster looks familiar, and he recognizes her as a slave driver from the quicksilver mines, but she was so low-level that he never learned her name. She shakes off the thought as all gnomes looking the same.

The group proceeds to the magistrate to buy a property. When they get there, they are told to store the non-humans in the cages at the front. An inquisitor leads the humans to the magistrate, on a throne. On top of the throne is a large human man, being fed by a gnome. Holliday fills him in on their story, and asks for a home in the craftsman district. The group had agreed on this district since non-humans could walk around a bit more freely while working. They buy a home for 2000g. Their non-human companions are released. On their way to their newly-acquired base, Branwyn sees a white raven fly overhead.
