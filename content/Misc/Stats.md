# Kill Count
> [!warning] WORK IN PROGRESS
> This section is not yet ready. Any information shown below may change, and is likely not actually accurate. I suggest you do not look into this at this time, as the stats may lead you to believe something that isn't true.

Below are the rules for kills. Skip over it if you don't care about the intricacies that go into this. There are basically only three rules, but I go into a lot of detail on why I count things the way I do. Although, I specially format my end decisions if you just want to look at the rules in a simple form.
### Rules
A "kill count" is hard to define. Should killing a rat be worth the same "number of kills" as killing a god? Should killing a rat even be worth a kill? If a group works together to kill a big bad, how are points allocated? It's difficult to consider, and there are a lot of intricacies we can ask about. But at the end of the day, I think there are two questions that are more important:

1. Why am I tracking this again? That is, what data is important to track, and what data do I, frankly, not care about?
2. What would make this data easy to track? I know easy data collection tends to be bad data collection, and good data collection tends to be hard data collection. However, this isn't being published, and "possible" data collection is better than data collection that I give up on.

To start off, I'm counting any kill. I'm thinking that if we count a horde of assassins in Session 31, why not count a horde of rats in Session 1? Functionally, in terms of how powerful a group is compared to us, I think we are basically presented with two enemy types: hordes and individual enemies. I think all hordes should be equal, at the end of the day, since they are a collection of enemies who are probably at the same level across all sessions, *relative to the players*.

The only issue I see is equating, like I said, a rat to a god. I don't want to look up a bunch of difficulty ratings, I don't feel comfortable saying "each fight is ten points split among its participants." No matter what I do to make the kills seem "fair," it means I have to put a lot of my own judgement and opinions which could muddy the data. I'm already being a bit subjective in terms of what I'm counting, like in terms of who's dead and who's unconscious, so I'm trying to avoid bringing in extra "hot takes."

All things considered, I think about kill counts for games, and that one kill count wiki I visit more than I have any right to. A video game kill count that pops up after a boss doesn't include difficulty level. That website doesn't include difficulty level. I'm just leaving it simple: take a life, get a point. This also makes it so easy for me.

However, one thing that I *can* control for is "kill stealing." We can create weighted kills, where anyone who assisted in the kill gets credit. While I could count who deals the final blow to get a "direct" kill counter, I feel like this is unfair. There are many characters who are more likely to get kills because of this, such as characters who can deal more damage, characters who exist more for support, characters who debuff or apply effects on enemies, etc.

All of this being said, I was originally going to count kills based on who dealt the final blow. I have all of that data, and it seems interest has been expressed in how the kill counts would be if we just discussed killing blows. So I'll add that data in.

> [!done] Rule 1: How Many Points Are Awarded
> Characters will earn a "weighted kill" determined by how much they contributed to the kill. Each character who contributed to the death will earn one point, divided by the number of characters who contributed.
> 
> Characters will earn a "killing blow" each time they deal the damage that finally kills a character.

I'm trying to be transparent on who does and doesn't count as being "dead." We knock a lot of people out, and there's the issue of characters being able to be turned undead, or being revived. I'm saying the character has to fully die. If they are *immediately* turned undead, and the death only occurred as a means of being turned undead, I'm not counting it. Revivals shouldn't undo the death.

> [!done] Rule 2: Who Can Award Points
> A character must die, and not simply be rendered unconscious, to be counted as a kill. If a character dies for the sole purpose of being turned undead, and maintains complex thought after being turned, it is not counted as a kill until the character dies.

There are also a lot of instances where two unnamed characters fight. I'm taking this on a case-by-case basis, but if it's clear that an unnamed character kills one person and then is never going to be seen again, I don't want them to bog down the table.

I want anyone who is important (as in, someone who I have made a notes page for) to be on this table. I also want anyone who killed one of those important characters to be on the list.

I want to include inconsequential characters if they do something I deem interesting or important enough to track, as well. Basically, I want a "cost of entry" for characters unrelated to the "important" characters. Right now, I'm setting the cost of entry at two, to remove anyone who kills someone and disappears forever. I may raise this if the list becomes too bogged down by unimportant characters with only one kill.

I ran into an issue where inconsequential characters kill a major character. For example, if one random thief kills off a named character, I'd like to include them. But if it's some unnamed knight in a group, like how Lexar died, I don't see a point in including them. The only way I could think to count this is by grouping them together as a horde, saying "this group of knights killed these five people," but since different knights likely claimed each person, I don't think I can include them. This is something I'll have to take on a case-by-case basis, likely, but you can see my reasoning in the text boxes for each case. For now, generally, we will say that I'll only include characters who have a character page.

In the case that there is a character who is tied to another character through some mechanic, such as a summon, I am not giving the summon points. Two notable examples of this is Jiren and Puck. If the character is unable to act independently of another, I think of them as an extension of whoever they follow. Also, they tend to be weak enough that they never get a final kill, and they would make it so both they and whoever they follow will have their weighted kills bogged down by another character.

> [!done] Rule 3: Who Can Get Points
> In order for a character to receive points, they must follow the rules below. If there are no characters who points can be given to per the rules below, the points are lost, not being awarded to anyone.
> 
> 1. The character and victim cannot be the same. No suicides or accidents will be counted.
> 2. The character cannot be part of a horde. For example, they cannot be one of many knights in an army, one of many animals in a pack, or other similar cases.
> 3. The character must be able to act independently rather than following another character. For example, Jiren and Puck cannot gain points.
> 4. The character must be important in some way. This can take many forms, but the most likely cases are below.
> 	- The character has a character page on this site.
> 	- The character is mysterious or important enough that it is believed they will *eventually* have a character page on this site.

>[!warning] Warning
>Note what I'm *not* making a rule. This is on purpose. The below cases should be understood.
>
>- Kills don't need to be earned "on-screen." We'll have to take characters at their word a bit, here. If a character claims to kill someone, or we hear a story of how a character dies, we have to believe it until proven otherwise. This gets difficult with fables and gods, but we do our best.
>- Kills don't need to be earned on a day on or after Session Zero. This is tricky, since we have generals and warlords. Remember that armies count as hordes, and are generally unable to earn points. Notably, this applies to flashbacks.
